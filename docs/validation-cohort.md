# Second Date — first 50-user validation cohort

## Objective
Determine whether the product creates a repeatable habit before investing in broader feature development or paid acquisition.

## Recruitment
Recruit 50 adults who already buy and open packaged food, beauty, wellness, or household products. Do not require participants to be sustainability enthusiasts. Avoid compensating people specifically for positive feedback.

## Onboarding task
Give each participant one instruction:

> Track five things you already have open.

Do not explain the interface beyond what is necessary to recover from a usability failure.

## Primary observations
1. Can the participant explain Second Date after seeing the landing page?
2. Do they create a first real item?
3. Do they create a second item without prompting?
4. Do they return after leaving the app?
5. Do reminders, when enabled, lead to a return or completion?
6. Do they record items as used or discarded?
7. Do they ask for a capability that is not present?

## Quantitative gates
Record:
- activation = users creating >=1 real item / cohort
- depth = median real items per activated user
- D7 retained use
- D30 retained use
- reminder adoption among eligible users
- reminder-to-return rate
- use-up rate = used / (used + discarded)
- account conversion
- Plus checkout intent and paid conversion when billing is enabled

These are measurement definitions, not predeclared success thresholds. Freeze thresholds before analyzing the completed cohort if a formal experiment is desired.

## Interviews
Interview at least 10 participants around days 7–14. Ask what they expected, what they forgot to track, what caused them to return, whether they would be disappointed if the product disappeared, and what they currently use instead.

## Decision rule
After 30 days, classify the bottleneck as acquisition, activation, retention, reminder utility, monetization, or product comprehension. The next roadmap tranche should target the measured bottleneck rather than the largest feature request.
